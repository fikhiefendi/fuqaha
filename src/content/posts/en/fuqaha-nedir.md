Anyone who works on Islamic law meets the same problem: learning about a single jurist means consulting five different *ṭabaqāt* works, and finding the scholarship written about him means searching separate catalogues in three languages.

**Fuqaha** was built to reduce that scatter. The site has three parts:

- **Jurists.** Biographies drawn from the *ṭabaqāt* and *tarājim* literature. Every statement is given with the volume and page of the book it comes from.
- **Map.** The places where jurists were born, studied, served as judges and died. Moving century by century, you can watch the centres of learning shift.
- **Bibliography.** Theses, articles, books and critical editions in Turkish, Arabic, English and other languages, filterable by type, language, year and subject.

As the archive grows, the records will be linked: a jurist's page will show his teachers and students, his places on the map, and the modern scholarship written about him.

Reporting errors and gaps is the most valuable contribution you can make.
